Chase "More" Menu Information Architecture

Revamped the "More" menu information architecture using user research and iterative design, boosting experience engagement by 124% and reducing bounce rate by 3%.

Background

Problem Problem

Our banking app needed navigation balancing quick access to specific services with discovery of valuable hidden features.

Solution Solution

Through iterative design cycles, user research insights and A/B testing validation, we optimized the IA to effectively solve navigation challenges.

Challenge Challenge

Designing navigation that meets immediate customer needs while strategically surfacing business-priority features to drive engagement and revenue growth.

Results Results

Achieved 124% increase in menu interactions and reduced bounce rate by 3%, demonstrating improved navigation effectiveness and user satisfaction.

Design for MVP

Initial information architecture based on Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) framework, user research insights, and first design iteration.

JTBD Information Architecture

Categorized user tasks into Primary Core (Pay & Transfer) and Engagement (Rewards & Shopping) JTBDs for strategic placement.

User Research

Analyzed user behavior patterns and navigation preferences to identify optimization opportunities.

First Design Iteration

Created initial menu structure with main categories: Pay & Transfer, Self-service, Travel & Redeem, Statements, Lock/Unlock Card, and More.

Data-Driven Strategy & Optimization

Transformed insights into action through systematic data collection, strategic iteration, and cognitive load reduction.

Collect & Analyze

Menu was mainly used to access servicing and travel features.

  • Pay & transfer had the highest access rate
  • Self-service & Travel & redeem had the highest lift
  • Statements & Lock/unlock card had the highest CTR

Iterate & Optimize

Servicing and travel sections moved to the top based on data insights.

  • Self-service section moved at the top for faster findability
  • Travel section moved above the fold for simpler discovery
  • Pay & transfer moved below the fold to learn customer dependency

Simplify Language

I simplified labels by removing unnecessary verbs and redundant words that create visual clutter without providing value.

Reduce Options

Through data analysis and app auditing, I eliminated the Pay & Transfer section, reducing customer choices without impacting stakeholders.

Label Optimization Examples

Strategic content simplification to reduce cognitive load and improve user experience.

How Does It Work?

The IA was carefully crafted by analyzing key Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) focused on findability, like Pay & transfer, alongside engagement JTBD aimed at discoverability, such as Track your finances. This strategic placement ensures these elements are front and center in the user interface.

step 1

Get Insights From Your Experiments

First experiment surprisingly showed us that beside the expected high engagement with Pay & transfer because at the top, the higher lift in terms of CTR was for another Promary Core JTBD - Self-service - and two of the Engagment sections - Redeem & shop and Travel & dining.

Pay & Transfer feature shows high engagement, but its visibility in Bottom Navigation is prominent.

Track your finances underperformed.

The Click-Through Rates (CTRs) for Self-service, Redeem & shop, and Travel & dining experienced a notable increase.

step 2

Update Your Information Architecture

Following our review of the initial results from the first round, we restructured the categories according to the data showing which sections our users accessed most often.

Following the initial experiment, it became evident that the categories of Self-service, Rewards & Shopping, and Travel & Dining have emerged as the primary focus areas.

Pay & transfer category is now centrally located on the screen, next to Track your finances, improving the user experience for managing customer transactions.

step 3

Work Hard On The Winning Variant

Following our review of the initial results from the first round, we restructured the categories according to the data showing which sections our users accessed most often. This resulted in a 124% increase in menu interactions and a 3% reduction in bounce rate.
